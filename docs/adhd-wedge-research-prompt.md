# Deep research: 10 ADHD wedges for OVOA, with the psychology behind each

You are a senior consumer-product strategist with a clinical psychology background. You know the adult ADHD research literature well: executive function, prospective memory, time perception, motivation and emotion regulation. You also know behavioural design. Do deep, sourced research and give me **10 specific wedges** for selling OVOA to adults with ADHD traits. For each one, show the psychological evidence for why it works.

I've already decided ADHD is the wedge. I'm not asking whether to pick it. I'm asking for the 10 sharpest ways in, the science that says each will work, and which 3 to build first.

---

## What OVOA is (as of 2026-10-01)

OVOA is a personal AI assistant from a small, early-stage company. It has real users in beta.

1. **Text OVOA (the front door).** An AI you text over iMessage, with nothing to install. It remembers things about you, sets reminders, runs routines, reads photos and voice notes, and can text you first.
2. **The OVOA app (iOS beta).** Voice talk, notes, Apple Health, a Day screen with routines and a to-do list, and full-screen nag alarms.
3. **OVOA Fit ($89.99).** A wristband with a button you press to talk to OVOA, plus heart rate.

**Pricing:** Free (app only, no AI). Base $9.95/mo. Plus $13.95/mo, which adds the background agent. Pro $25.95/mo.

**Trial:** text the number and get 15 free texts with no sign-up. After that, OVOA sends one text with a link to pay for Base.

### What already works vs what's missing (be honest about this gap)
**Built:**
- Reminders by text.
- "Urgent" reminders that nag every 2 min until you say "done".
- Routines (medication, habits) with a re-buzz, then a "Did you do it? Text done or snooze" text.
- Streaks.
- A nightly auto-built to-do list (up to 10 items).
- "You said you'd…" nudges on promises, which need Plus.
- A follow-up when you leave a question hanging, which needs Plus.
- Quiet hours.
- A cap of 12 unprompted texts a day, with back-off when you stop replying.

**Missing:**
- A normal reminder fires once and is never followed up.
- Reminders can't be listed, moved or cancelled by text.
- There's no breaking a task into steps and no "what should I do right now".
- The morning brief and evening list only reach people who installed the app, not text-only users.
- Medication routines skip the "did you take it?" text.

### Hard constraints (don't recommend around these)
- **Zero setup.** No extra hardware beyond OVOA Fit and no config. OAuth integrations are OK.
- **No always-listening and no ambient recording sent to servers.** Capture only happens when the user acts. So body doubling can't mean an open mic.
- **iMessage only for texting.** No SMS and no group chats yet.
- **Small ad budget:** a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, plus organic.
- **Meta's personal-attributes policy.** Ads can't say or imply the viewer has ADHD ("Got ADHD?" and "your ADHD brain" are banned). They can describe the behaviour ("If you've ever set 6 alarms for one thing…"). Landing pages and organic posts have more room.
- **We are not a medical product.** No diagnosis, treatment or clinical claims. Medication support is reminders only.

**Competitors in this space** (re-verify every fact):
- Tiimo, Inflow, Focusmate, Structured, Llama Life, Goblin Tools, Motion, Todoist, Due, Routinery and Fabulous.
- Fieldy, which runs long-lived "ADHD reminders AI"-style Meta ads that never say ADHD.
- Siri in iOS 27, ChatGPT tasks and memory, and Poke.
- Human ADHD coaches and body-doubling services.

## The question

**What are the 10 most specific situations where an adult with ADHD traits would pay $9.95–$13.95/month for an assistant that texts them first and won't let things drop? What does the psychology say about why each one works?**

A good wedge:
- names a **specific person in a specific moment**, e.g. "the night before a deadline they haven't started", not "people with ADHD"
- targets a **named mechanism** from the research, such as:
  - prospective memory failure
  - time blindness or poor time perception
  - trouble starting tasks
  - delay aversion
  - out of sight, out of mind (object permanence)
  - shame and avoidance loops
  - working memory overload
  - hyperfocus
  - interest-driven motivation
  - decision paralysis
- shows value **inside the 15-text trial**
- can be **advertised under Meta's policy**
- **spreads** naturally: a partner, a roommate or an accountability buddy sees it
- doesn't wear off after week 2, which is the classic death of ADHD apps

## Research to do

### 1. The psychology, with real evidence
For each mechanism you use, cite peer-reviewed work and grade the evidence:
- **Strong:** replicated, meta-analysis
- **Moderate:** a few controlled studies
- **Weak:** small or uncontrolled studies
- **Folk:** popular in the community, with little or no research

Be strict. "Body doubling", "dopamine menu" and "interest-based nervous system" may be folk. Say so if they are, and say whether that matters for selling. Cover at least these:
- **External cues for prospective memory in adult ADHD.** Do reminders from outside actually help, and which kinds?
- **Implementation intentions** ("when X, I will Y"). Gollwitzer and the ADHD-specific studies.
- **Time perception and time blindness** in ADHD (Barkley and others). What fixes help?
- **Starting tasks:** how small the first step should be, commitment devices and accountability.
- **Reminder fatigue:** when do repeated nudges stop working or start to annoy? What does notification research say about timing, variety and how often to nudge?
- **Shame, rejection sensitivity and avoidance.** How should a follow-up be worded after a miss so it pulls the person back instead of making them ignore it?
- **Novelty and habit.** Why ADHD users drop apps, and what keeps them engaged (variety, social accountability, low friction).
- **Social accountability and someone else being present.** What's real, and can a text-based check-in reproduce any of it without a live mic?

### 2. Voice of customer
- Mine r/ADHD, r/adhdwomen, r/ADHD_Programmers, r/adhd_anxiety, App Store reviews of the competitors above, and ADHD TikTok/YouTube comments.
- Collect the exact words people use for each pain, especially "I wish something would just ___" and "I've tried every app and ___".
- Quote short snippets with links and dates.

### 3. Willingness to pay
- What do adults with ADHD already pay for? Coaching rates, Focusmate, Tiimo, Inflow, planners and virtual assistants.
- Use real prices with sources.
- Which pains show up next to spending, and which only show up as complaints?

### 4. Ten wedges
For each one, give:
- **Who and when:** one sentence naming the person and the moment.
- **Mechanism:** the psychological cause, with citations and an evidence grade.
- **What OVOA does by text:** the actual exchange, written as a 4–6 message iMessage thread. Mark which parts work today and which need building (use the built/missing lists above).
- **Why the psychology says it works:** the mechanism, plus the design rule it implies. For example: "the follow-up must offer a smaller next step, not repeat the original ask".
- **Why free tools fail here:** Siri, ChatGPT, Apple Reminders and alarms.
- **The aha moment inside 15 texts:** which text number it lands on.
- **Meta-safe ad hook:** behaviour only, no condition named. Also a landing-page line, where ADHD can be named.
- **How it spreads.**
- **Risk:** what could make it backfire, such as annoyance, shame or feeling surveilled.

Include non-obvious ones. Examples, not a limit:
- the bills and admin pile ("the wall of awful")
- appointments made and then forgotten
- replying to texts and emails you've been avoiding
- starting the thing at all
- getting out the door on time
- the medication or refill loop
- a partner who's tired of being the reminder
- a student before a deadline
- returns, cancellations and other small chores
- "the dump at 1am": getting everything out of your head before bed

### 5. Score all 10
Score each from 1 to 5 on:
- pain intensity
- strength of the psychological evidence
- willingness to pay
- how clearly OVOA beats free tools
- time to aha within 15 texts
- whether it lasts past week 2
- virality
- how well it fits the constraints
- build cost given what already exists

Then **pick the top 3** and give the strongest case against each one.

### 6. Design rules
Write a short list of **must** and **never** rules for how OVOA should talk to someone with ADHD traits. Ground each rule in the evidence from section 1. Cover:
- how often to chase
- how a follow-up should be worded after a miss
- when to give up on something
- how many items to show at once
- how to handle a long silence
- how to avoid feeling like a nagging parent

## Deliverable

1. **The answer first, in under 150 words:** the top 3 wedges and why.
2. **The 10 wedges**, in the format from section 4.
3. **The scored table.**
4. **An evidence appendix:** each mechanism, its key citations and its grade.
5. **The design rules** from section 6.
6. **The trial script:** the first text OVOA sends, plus how the 15 trial texts should be steered for the top wedge.
7. **A 2-week validation plan under $500.** For each of the top 3: the exact test, the metric and the go/kill threshold. Example metric: "% of trial users who reply 'done' to a follow-up within their 15 texts".
8. **What to stop saying** on ovoa.ai and in ads because it dilutes the ADHD wedge.
9. **Sources** with dates.

**Rules:**
- Cite every factual claim. Mark anything you inferred as **(inference)**.
- Don't invent statistics or studies. If the research doesn't exist, say so and say how we'd test it ourselves.
- Separate what peer-reviewed science shows from what the ADHD community believes. Both matter, for different reasons.
- Make no medical or treatment claims.
- Be blunt. If a popular idea has weak evidence, or would annoy the people it's meant to help, say so.
- Don't recommend anything that breaks the constraints above.
